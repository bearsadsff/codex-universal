What motivates students to use generative AI? Of course, the general idea that xyz generation is lazy, unmotivated and always willing to take the path of least resistance is popular. While that may be true for some, there are students with a deep motivation to learn and grow. These students exist in a world that continues to present obstacles for learning. People are working more and making less, they are working jobs well below their skillset just to ensure they maintain their standard of living. At the same time, the cost of living has increased considerably. Health and wellbeing are less and less accessible.

Notably, students are working more hours than advised while also pursuing their education. The need for shortcuts to ensure they meet their financial targets and their educational goals can be helped by generative AI. Learning feels so much easier and accessible with AI. Tools like CliffNotes, Reddit and Quora helped to answer specific questions or provide quick information, a better alternative to reading through the complex language of academic literature. AI is a tool that offers further simplicity to mitigate knowledge deficits.

Students are also pressured by the negative news surrounding their future prospects in the job market. The use of AI seems to be an essential skill and yet they are receiving mixed policies on how to integrate these tools with their education. With the feeling that you have to have technical skills to survive the competitive job market, you may be inclined to take shortcuts to bolster your options. Burn-out is consistently reported to be at all time highs. Students and working professionals feel like they have less and less time to dedicate to tasks that matter.

Although there are valid reasons for students to not use AI tools, there must be an understanding that they are not malicious or morally bankrupt. Antagonizing students damages their relationships with learning and educators. They are facing obstacles that do not leave room to stop and think. Designing solutions start from classroom policies that account for the different walks of life students come from. These policies should be clear on what ways AI may or may not be used, and what to do when feeling like they are not able to meet deadlines or benchmarks. They should also be consistent across classes. Consistency ensures there is no confusion when dealing with Honor Code violations.

Media literacy sessions can help students also understand WHY using AI may not be the best idea. Concrete examples of the ways AI can misinterpret and hallucinate important information can guide students to be more hands-on even when using AI tools. They can also learn about better ways AI can be used for their professional development. Pessimistic students may respond better when they feel like they are learning real-world skills, especially when AI feels like it's the most important thing to learn today. All that being said, this essay was written from the perspective of a student who used AI tools, got the lowest grade in that course and never used it again for education. After re-taking the course with a better professor and a less chaotic personal life, I was able to actually fill in my knowledge gaps without a crutch. I did not use AI for every course, just that one where I felt hopeless and did not want to fail. And I have had similar conversations with fellow students. Hope is not lost!
